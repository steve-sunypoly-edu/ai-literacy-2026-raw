The conversation moved through several distinct phases: Claude first responded to an email exchange [The author] was having with a colleague ([Colleague], a writing professor) about AI and writing pedagogy, offering analysis through a hypertext/Ted Nelson lens. [The author] then refined his own email responses with Claude's input, ultimately deciding to hold his position and not over-explain. He is developing the Learner's Permit (a 15-exercise scaffold) and an AI First-Year Experience (AI-FYE) pilot cohort architecture. The conversation closed with [The author] sharing a generated literature review paragraph attributed to outside scholars; Claude identified it as an example of the exact problem the framework's provenance note warns against — authoritative-sounding text with unverifiable citations, softened claims, and a performed scholarly structure that substitutes for actual argument.
