AI-FYE document structure and course design

Conversation Overview

[The author] is working as a SUNY AI Fellow for the Public Good, developing an AI literacy curriculum framework in response to SUNY Board Resolution 2024-64, which mandates AI literacy integration into general education by Fall 2026. He has been building a comprehensive AI literacy collection that includes frameworks like the "Learner's Permit" (LP) and "Commercial Driver's License" (CDL), and is now developing an "AI-FYE" (AI in the First Year Experience) program. [The author]'s approach to AI literacy centers on "reading, thinking, and writing using the tools, techniques, and technological systems consistent with one's peers" (RTW) - treating AI as the medium through which literate practice happens rather than as subject matter.

The conversation focused on creating a faculty invitation document for the AI-FYE pilot program. [The author] outlined a specific architecture: 20 students in a pilot cohort taking AI 188 (a 1-credit "AI Salon" course) as a co-requisite with general education and major courses. The AI Salon would examine AI-assisted artifacts students produce across disciplines, creating cross-disciplinary comparison opportunities. [The author] emphasized that participating faculty keep their existing courses and assignments, only coordinating timing on key assignments. The program begins with a shared orientation day where all students and faculty complete the Learner's Permit together.

[The author] demonstrated his collaborative approach to document development, noting that this document itself was "developed through sustained conversation with an AI system - generating design specs, working through multiple versions, validating and verifying across models" with the model getting "the last word" on outputs. He provided a concrete example of this iterative process: when an early reviewer suggested that 2-credit courses were too narrow for general education, "one prompt, one iteration" changed the entire program design. [The author] added a "What's Next" section explaining that participating faculty would join the project through a shared Claude Team Project to continue iterating and generating necessary pilot artifacts, with an additional track for developing grant proposals for Fall 2027 implementation. He also specified that student artifacts would be managed through Microsoft SharePoint with granular security controls, and emphasized that the program is "AI-forward by design" - faculty who join have already agreed to AI engagement as a selection condition.
